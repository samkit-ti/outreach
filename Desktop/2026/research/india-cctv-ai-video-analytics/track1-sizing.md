## 3. CCTV installed base in India

### National
- **[FACT]** CP Plus MD Aditya Khemka: India sells ~45M cameras/year, to cross 50M in 2025, ~70M by end of decade; CP Plus ~33% share (IFSEC India, 2025-05-17). https://ifsecindia.com/Post.aspx?Id=45969
- **[FACT]** Counterpoint/ELCINA: AI-capable camera shipments 7.7M (2025) → 9.6M (2026) → 27.8M (2031); IP ≈ 60% of unit sales in 2026 (secondary summaries of the Sept 2026 report). https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/indias-cctv-market-to-more-than-double-to-%E2%82%B9246b-by-fy29/
- **[FACT]** Counterpoint (2025-05-06): "IP cameras already make up more than two-thirds of the overall market" (by value); government procurement = 30–35% of CCTV demand. https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/cctv-firms-brace-for-disruption-as-india-tightens-regulations
- **[FACT]** Mordor: analog still ~52% of unit installed base in 2024 (analog camera sub-report); IP 54.7% of 2025 value. https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-surveillance-analog-camera-market
- **[FACT, low quality]** Blog/aggregator claims of "75–85M cameras installed annually" (cctvhubtech, cofacto) are unsourced and conflict with the CP Plus figure; treat as unreliable. https://cctvhubtech.com/how-many-cctv-cameras-in-india/
- **[ESTIMATE]** Installed base: 45–50M units/yr × 5–6 yr useful life, minus churn → **~180–250M cameras installed** nationally (2026). No government or analyst publishes a national total; Smart Cities Mission reports only 83,000+ cameras under ICCCs (PIB, 2025). https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?NoteId=154736&ModuleId=3&reg=3&lang=1
- **[ESTIMATE]** AI-capable share of installed base ≈ 7.7M+9.6M+(prior years ~8M) ≈ 25M / 200M = **~10–12%** by end-2026. Share actually connected to any paid analytics/VMS backend is far lower — see §5.

**STQC / MeitY Essential Requirements (ER) impact**
- **[FACT]** From 2025-04-09 only STQC-certified IP cameras may be sold; absolute deadline 2026-04-01; analog exempt. As of 2025-02-29 only CP Plus, Prama, Matrix, Sparsh, Qubo had compliant models; by July 2025 only 17 models certified vs 350+ in queue (91 Capital). Chinese brands effectively excluded from April 2026 (Business Standard, 2026-03). https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/cctv-firms-brace-for-disruption-as-india-tightens-regulations ; https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/india-ban-chinese-cctv-government-security-concerns-hikvision-dahua-market-126033000316_1.html
- **[FACT]** Result: Indian brands' IP camera revenue share 27% → 73% FY25→FY26; Indian players >80% of CCTV market by Feb 2026; short-term shipment dip and entry-level price increase (Khemka), then expected normalisation. Counterpoint's 34% CAGR to FY29 is largely a value-mix effect (certified IP cameras cost more) plus pent-up demand.
- **[INFERENCE]** For a software founder: ER compliance has (a) consolidated the camera layer around ~5 Indian OEMs who all now push their own edge-AI and cloud apps, (b) made government buyers insist on India-hosted, certified stacks — a moat for Indian software vendors vs Hikvision/Dahua software, and (c) temporarily raised hardware prices, squeezing budget available for analytics in SMB deals.

## 6. Contrarian evidence

1. **Analytics is becoming a free camera feature.** Hikvision AcuSense ships on every DS-2CD2xxx G2 camera and recent DS-76xx NVRs, licence-free per channel; AcuSense 3.0 + AcuSearch (2024–25) adds attribute forensic search in the NVR. Dahua WizSense/SMD Plus from ~INR 3,000/camera adds human/vehicle classification, face, people counting, heat maps. CP Plus now markets "edge AI cameras that decide" with human/vehicle detection built in (Khemka interview 2026-07-10). https://netviewcctv.co.uk/blog/hikvision-acusense-guide ; https://zbotic.in/hikvision-vs-dahua-cctv-india-comparison-2026/ ; https://www.gadgetbridge.com/interviewed-aditya-khemka-from-cp-plus-on-merging-ai-with-cctv-security/
2. **Global baseline: ~80% of cameras shipped in 2024 already carried analytics (23% rules-based, ~2/3 deep-learning)** — Axis citing industry data. Perimeter/intrusion/people-count — the "Essential"/"Professional" tiers in Indian price lists — are exactly what the camera now does for free. https://www.axis.com/explore/future-of-video-surveillance
3. **Indian pure-plays have thin revenue despite marquee logos.** Wobot: INR 13.3 Cr FY25 after ~6 years and Sequoia/Peak XV backing; "a marquee logo is not the same as a large revenue line" (The Invincible India, 2026-07-29). Staqu INR 19.6 Cr. Videonetics, the largest VMS/analytics pure-play, is ~INR 79 Cr after 17 years. https://theinvincibleindia.in/wobot-ai-deep-dive/
4. **Software is a small slice of the Indian stack.** Counterpoint/ELCINA: IP cameras + NVRs = 85% of CCTV revenue; Mordor: hardware 63%; IMARC's entire India video analytics market (USD 316M) is ~7% of Mordor's surveillance total. Government, 30–35% of demand, buys via L1 SI tenders where analytics is a line item, not a product.
5. **Price points are collapsing toward storage economics.** Indian analytics tiers at INR 250–350/cam/month (Yotta) vs USD 20–90 globally; integrators report customers benchmark VSaaS against generic cloud storage (cctvwiki 2025-12). Entry cloud recording at INR 290/cam/month leaves little room for an analytics premium in SMB.
6. **Installed base quality.** Large shares of public cameras are analog/defunct (Chennai Third Eye shelved; Delhi auditing 2.6 lakh cameras; Delhi replacing 1.4 lakh Phase-1 cameras) — connected, analysable cameras are a fraction of headline counts.
7. **Policy risk cuts both ways.** STQC/ER excluded Chinese OEM software and raised hardware prices in 2025–26; the same regulatory muscle could mandate certified/India-hosted analytics stacks (good for local vendors) or push analytics onto certified edge devices (bad for standalone cloud analytics).

## 7. Triangulated estimate — India AI video analytics software, 2026

**TAM (all AI video analytics software + analytics services, India, 2026):**
- Top-down: IMARC USD 316M (2025) × 1.13 = USD 358M; M&M path ≈ USD 470M; Mordor-implied (4.84B × ~8% software/analytics share) ≈ USD 390M.
- Bottom-up: commercial realised ~USD 30M + government/infra analytics ~USD 60–110M + OEM/VMS software and services ≈ USD 150–250M → USD 240–390M.
- **Best estimate: USD 350M (INR ~2,900 Cr), range USD 280–450M**, growing 13–20%/yr. Software-only (ex-services) ≈ USD 250M.

**SAM for a new standalone analytics product selling to commercial sites (retail, logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, education, residential), excluding government SI tenders:**
- Full-attach ceiling ≈ 2.7M addressable cameras × INR 6,000/yr ≈ USD 190M; realised 2026 ≈ USD 30–50M; reachable in 3–4 years at 2x attach and flat ARPU ≈ **USD 80–120M**.
- Government/infra analytics (USD 60–110M/yr) is only reachable as a sub-vendor to SIs/RailTel/OEMs.

**Key assumptions to stress-test:** (a) ARPU holds at INR 500/cam/month against free OEM analytics; (b) attach rates double by 2029; (c) installed base ~200M with ~10% AI-capable; (d) IMARC's 72% software share applies; (e) government analytics = 10–15% of tender value.
